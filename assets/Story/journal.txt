# Grave Reckoning: the Journal. Plain text, edit freely.
#
#   entry <id>
#   style  parchment | clipping | scrawl | tally | record
#                                      (letters and ledgers | newspaper | the investigator's own hand | a count)
#                                      record: no text lines; the page fills itself in from the save (the investigator's running tally)
#   title  <heading>
#   source <who wrote it, or where it was found>
#   unlock free | chapter N | finale | graves N | lanterns N | deed KIND N
#                                      (chapter N: settle that chapter's last grave; graves N: settle N graves
#                                       in total on any vigil; lanterns N: put out N lanterns)
#                                      deed KIND N: something the player did, counted quietly. KIND is one of
#                                       stumbles (pursuers who walked into an open grave), offerings (flames
#                                       gathered), flawless (graves settled without losing a life), shut
#                                       (times "The Way Is Shut" was proved), rite (most pursuers laid to
#                                       rest together in one rite) and hard (chapters settled on the hardest
#                                       vigil, The Faithful).
#   book   casebook | earlier          (casebook, the default: the investigator's own pages. earlier: pages
#                                       from the hands that held this book before, bound in; shown in the
#                                       Journal's second book, L1 / R1 to turn to it)
#   era    <year>                      (earlier pages: sets the paper and the ink; 1264 vellum, 1587 brown
#                                       paper, 1741 laid paper, 1864 a notebook)
#   margin <hand> <words>      (a note in another keeper's handwriting, below the page text; hand is
#                               teacher (blue-black ink, brisk), elder (faded brown, small) or pencil (grey
#                               pencil, a sceptic). They question and argue with the page, and with the
#                               investigator's own notes; they never settle anything. Keep each short;
#                               a page that is already full drops its notes (the text audit says so))
#   hint   <text>                      (shown under a locked page; the older hands leave one, in their own
#                                       voice, as the only clue to what unlocks it. Nothing else says so)
#   text   <a paragraph>               (as many as fit; about 600 characters in all)
#
# Redaction inside a paragraph:  [[struck through, still readable]]  and
# {{blacked out with ink}}. The words inside {{ }} are never shown; they only set
# the length of the bar, so a row of x's will do.
# Wear: ((worn away)). The book's own entropy: the words are simply gone, with a faint stain
# and a gap the length of the words inside. Use it very sparsely (about one page in four),
# never on a word the page cannot do without, and never on the investigator's own pages: they
# are fresh. Decide each gap by hand so wear cannot swallow a key word.
#
# The book is a community's, passed down: lent, never given, from a teacher who asks after the
# dead of this district to the protege who takes it on (the player). Earlier keepers have bound in
# what they found, which is why the second book has pages from 1264, 1587, 1741 and 1864, the
# four years the oldest registers mark. They are written the way their century wrote, and each
# understands the Inheritance in its own terms (a judgement, witchcraft, a distemper of the blood,
# a nervous condition) and none of them is right. Each ends badly. None says it is a cycle, none
# is named, and the 1264 leaf is only a distant atrocity that a later keeper copied in for one word.
#
# The Journal is the casebook of an unnamed investigator, a Victorian
# correspondent who is following the story, probably to their own grave. The
# printed pages are what they collected. The scrawl pages, and the marks that
# crowd the margins of every page as the player settles graves, are their own
# hand, steadily less steady. Never name them.
#
# The entries are shown in the order they appear here, which is the order of
# the story, so the Journal reads as one account however the pages were found.
# Rules from the lore: the young man's death is never shown; his name is never
# given (it is torn away or left blank); nobody states what the cycle is.
